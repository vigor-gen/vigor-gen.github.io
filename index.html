Abstract

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) achieves strong sample efficiency by planning within learned latent dynamics, yet its performance degrades substantially under unseen visual distractions such as background variations, lighting changes, or camera shifts. Unlike model-free RL, where encoder perturbations affect only single-step predictions, MBRL suffers from a two-level vulnerability: visual distractions first push encoder outputs out of distribution, and these errors then compound through recursive latent rollouts over the planning horizon.

We propose VIsual Generalization via latent-space cOnsistency in model-based RL (VIGOR), a framework that enables zero-shot generalization to unseen visual distractions while retaining the sample efficiency of its MBRL backbone. VIGOR enforces latent-space consistency through three interdependent components: (i) asymmetric weak-to-strong augmentation, which pairs weak-only and weak-to-strong latent views within a single batch; (ii) dynamics-level consistency, which enforces augmentation-invariant transition predictions through direct latent regression; and (iii)encoder-level stabilization, which prevents encoder drift under the cross-augmentation supervision imposed by dynamics-level consistency.

Evaluations on the DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) and Robosuite show that VIGOR, instantiated on TD-MPC2, outperforms state-of-the-art model-free and model-based baselines in zero-shot generalization, surpassing the second-best baseline by 3.4% on DMC and 43.6% on Robosuite. Moreover, VIGOR achieves these improvements while maintaining sample efficiency comparable to that of its backbone and requiring fewer environment interactions than the other evaluated baselines. Ablations further show that VIGOR's robustness is augmentation-agnostic: replacing the default augmentation with alternatives from distinct perturbation families preserves strong generalization, confirming that latent-space consistency, not the augmentation choice, drives robustness. Finally, a second instantiation of VIGOR on DreamerV3, a structurally distinct recurrent state-space world model, also improves zero-shot generalization over the DreamerV3 baseline on both benchmarks, suggesting that the benefits of VIGOR are not limited to the TD-MPC2 backbone.

The Problem: A Two-Level Vulnerability

Latent-space MBRL plans inside a learned world model. An encoder \(h_\theta\) maps pixels to a latent state, and a dynamics model \(d_\theta\) rolls that state forward to evaluate future actions. This works well as long as test observations look like training observations. The video below walks through what happens when they do not.

In model-free RL, a visual shift perturbs only a single-step prediction. In model-based RL the same shift causes two failures in sequence:

  1. Encoder-level drift. Visual distractions yield out-of-distribution latents \(z'_t \notin \mathcal{Z}^{\text{train}}\), even though the physical state is unchanged.
  2. Dynamics-level error accumulation. The dynamics model propagates these latents recursively over the planning horizon \(H\), so errors compound across steps and the rollouts used for planning diverge.

Techniques designed for single-step robustness are therefore insufficient. Our goal is zero-shot generalization: no test-time data and no adaptation, and only appearance-based distractions change between training and deployment.

From Problem to Solution

If the failure happens at two levels, the fix has to act at two levels. The world model should predict the same transitions regardless of the appearance-based perturbation applied to its input, and the shared encoder should remain stable while this constraint is imposed.

Sample Efficiency

Robustness comes at negligible cost. VIGOR's learning curves closely track those of the unmodified backbone on both TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3. On Robosuite, TD-MPC2 and TD-MPC2+VIGOR converge at comparable rates, while the other baselines fall behind in both speed and asymptotic returns.

Latent-Space Consistency

VIGOR is consistent at both levels of the vulnerability. Rolling out each world model from identical inputs shows that VIGOR's initial encodings stay close to their clean counterparts, and this alignment is preserved through the rollout horizon. TD-MPC2 and Dr. G produce scattered initial encodings or divergent rollouts, with degraded returns.
